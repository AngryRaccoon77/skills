# Analysis Playbook

## Equity checklist

Do not use a rigid point score as a substitute for analysis. Cover the factors that are material to the issuer.

### Earnings and cash flow
- Revenue and operating profit direction.
- Free cash flow and cash conversion.
- One-off items versus recurring earnings.
- Capex intensity and maintenance versus growth capex.

### Balance sheet
- Net debt and leverage appropriate to the sector.
- Debt maturity/refinancing profile.
- Interest-rate sensitivity.
- Liquidity and access to funding.

### Valuation
Use multiple lenses when possible:
- P/E or earnings yield for suitable companies.
- EV/EBITDA for capital structures where it is informative.
- P/B and ROE for banks/financials where appropriate.
- FCF yield for cash-generative companies.
- Dividend yield only after testing payout sustainability.

Compare valuation with the company's own history, sector peers, and a plausible range of future earnings. Do not treat a low multiple as proof of cheapness.

### Dividends
Check:
- formal dividend policy;
- payout base and constraints;
- debt/capex needs;
- controlling shareholder incentives and governance;
- whether the dividend is covered by sustainable cash flow.

### Russia-specific risk mapping
When relevant, analyze:
- domestic versus export revenue;
- RUB sensitivity;
- oil/gas/metals/commodity sensitivity;
- sanctions and settlement infrastructure;
- import dependence and equipment/technology restrictions;
- regulated tariffs/prices;
- state ownership and policy constraints;
- tax/mineral extraction/export-duty changes;
- free float and trading liquidity.

## Bank equity checklist

For banks, prioritize sector-specific metrics rather than generic industrial multiples:
- ROE / profitability quality.
- Net interest margin and rate sensitivity.
- Cost of risk and asset quality.
- Capital adequacy and buffers.
- Loan/deposit growth and funding structure.
- Dividend capacity subject to capital needs and regulation.

## Bond checklist

### Cash-flow terms
Verify:
- ISIN / exact issue.
- Nominal and current clean/dirty price conventions.
- Coupon rate and frequency.
- Accrued coupon.
- Maturity.
- Offer/call dates.
- Amortization schedule.
- Floating-rate formula if applicable.

### Return
Prefer yield to maturity / yield to offer and spread analysis over coupon rate.
Compare against:
- OFZ of similar duration;
- other high-quality corporate issues;
- money-market alternatives for short horizons.

### Credit
Review:
- current external ratings when available;
- leverage and interest coverage;
- liquidity/refinancing schedule;
- parent guarantees or collateral if any;
- subordination/perpetual features;
- sector cyclicality and sanctions/regulatory exposure.

### Rate risk
Fixed-rate long-duration bonds can gain when yields fall and lose when yields rise. Floaters reduce some duration risk but can lose income if reference rates fall. Do not assume either structure is always better; fit it to the current portfolio and scenario set.

## Panic-day protocol

When the Russian market falls sharply:
1. Identify the specific trigger and verify it from fresh sources.
2. Separate market-wide repricing from issuer-specific damage.
3. Check whether expected cash flows, access to financing, settlement, ownership rights, or regulation materially changed.
4. Check portfolio concentration before buying the dip.
5. Re-estimate expected return using the new price and updated fundamentals.
6. If the thesis is intact and the portfolio is underweight, staged buying can be reasonable.
7. If facts are unclear, preserve liquidity rather than forcing a decision.

A sharp decline is not itself a buy signal or a sell signal.
